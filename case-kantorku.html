Modeling acquisition for a low-ticket B2B SaaS

Self-directed modeling exercise · Meta Ads funnel design, CPL diagnosis, CAC/LTV

What this is: a modeling exercise I completed on my own time, solving a third-party brief for a B2B HRIS product. KantorKu is not my company and I have not operated its ads. What this demonstrates is the method, not a client result — the brief supplied the constraints, and the modeling is mine.

The brief: given Rp100,000,000/month in Meta budget, a Rp50,000 target CPL, a 10% landing page conversion rate, and a 25% SQL rate, produce a defensible acquisition plan and a target CAC for a product priced under Rp3 million per month.

That is a small budget against a SaaS price point, which makes the funnel model the whole argument. Here is the model.

Volume the funnel backwards from CAC

Starting at CPL Rp50,000 and working down to revenue:

StageMathMonthly
Leads100,000,000 ÷ 50,0002,000
Landing page views needed2,000 ÷ 10% CVR20,000
SQLs2,000 × 25% SQL rate500
Paying customers at Rp1.2M CAC100,000,000 ÷ 1,200,000~83

The implied SQL→customer conversion is 16.6%, which is where the model gets interesting: that number is not a marketing assumption, it is a sales and demo capacity assumption. If the demo team can only run 60 closes a month, the CAC the budget actually delivers is Rp1.67M, not Rp1.2M. The bottleneck moves downstream of the ad account.

Budget allocation across funnel stages

StageShareCampaign goalPurpose
Prospecting35% · Rp35MReach, video views, trafficBuild the top-of-funnel audience, lookalike seeds, and retargeting pools
Consideration30% · Rp30MLanding page views, engagementChase LP views and CVR to benchmark creative and landing page quality
Retargeting20% · Rp20MRetargeting visitors, video engagers, past leadsConvert warm audience into SQLs and demo bookings, excluding converters
Lead gen & conversion15% · Rp15MInstant forms, direct WhatsApp, web conversionCapture high-intent leads; higher CPC accepted to remove conversion friction

The weighting is deliberate: 85% goes to demand creation and capture, 15% to bottom-funnel conversion. Inverting that ratio is the most common error in low-CAC-count accounts — you cannot retarget an audience you have not built.

Campaign structure: 11–12 campaigns, split by creative

Each funnel stage is divided so learnings arrive fast and winning creative is isolatable.

  • Prospecting (3). 1% lookalike of best customers/SQLs first — historically the best performer. One interest campaign (HR, payroll, productivity, UMKM) for scale. One vertical-video awareness campaign to feed retargeting.
  • Consideration (3). One campaign per creative variant — video hero, static value proposition, carousel demo — so the winning angle is identifiable and can be scaled on CVR lift rather than on CTR.
  • Retargeting (3–4). Segmented by recency and intent: 7-day landing page visitors, video viewers at 25–75% and engagers at 7–30 days, form openers and abandoners, and past leads. Each gets different creative — urgency, case study, demo call-to-action — because a repeat of a prospecting ad to a warm audience wastes the impression.
  • Lead gen & conversion (2–3). Meta Lead Ads instant forms and direct WhatsApp remove the friction of a web form. Web conversion campaigns feed the lookalike audience.

Formats tested: Reels/Shorts at 15–45 seconds, single image, and carousel. The pain-point and demo angles are the ones I would build first, because they target the specific failure that makes a prospect look up a competitor.

Diagnosing a 35% CPL rise inside 48 hours

This is the question that separates a media buyer from a campaign operator. Fast mitigation first — pause the lowest-CTR ad sets, refresh creative, cut daily spend on non-performing ad sets — then the actual diagnosis, in this order:

1. Tracking and web events

Verify the pixel and Conversions API are firing, and check recent changes to them. Confirm lead forms, direct WhatsApp, and CTAs still submit.

This is first because of a specific failure mode: if the conversion event breaks, tracked conversions fall, and the algorithm bids as if CPL genuinely rose — while real performance is unchanged. You can burn a week optimizing against a number that is broken.

2. Creative and audience signals

Check CTR, CPC, frequency, and relevance ranking. Falling CTR or frequency above 2.5–3 is creative fatigue, and it needs new creative or a new audience, not a bid change.

3. Auction and bidding

Check CPM and CPC. A significant rise points to competitor movement or a seasonal event. Mitigate by reducing budget temporarily, shifting spend to campaigns with better CPA, or narrowing targeting.

Also ruled out in parallel: landing page and site health, CTA, and policy or disapprovals.

The CAC question

Target CAC of Rp1,200,000 is defensible for this product, with an ideal band of Rp500,000–2,000,000. The assumptions that produce it:

  • Price Rp15,000 per employee per month
  • Average client 30–50 employees
  • ARPU Rp450,000–750,000 per month
  • Gross margin 50% (SaaS)

At Rp450,000 ARPU, annual revenue is Rp5.4M and gross profit ~Rp2.7M. Over a three-year retention, LTV is Rp8.1M — an LTV/CAC ratio of 6.75. CAC stays healthy even with severe churn, because a customer who stays only one year still clears acquisition cost. There is headroom below Rp1.2M to optimize further.

What this exercise demonstrates

Not that I have run this account — I have not. That I can take a business constraint set, invert the funnel from an economic target, allocate budget against a stage-weighted model, structure campaigns so learnings arrive within days rather than weeks, and distinguish a tracking failure from a genuine auction problem inside a 48-hour window. Those are the parts of performance marketing that transfer to any account.
